AI Analysis Summary

This lengthy honorific decree from Sestos provides a detailed account of the career of Menas, son of Menes, during the late 2nd century BC. It covers his diplomatic missions to kings and Roman generals, his financial generosity during Thracian raids and food shortages, his religious service as a priest of King Attalus, and his two terms as gymnasiarch. Notably, it records the city's transition to a local bronze currency and Menas's role in managing the mint. The text is a prime example of Hellenistic civic euergetism and the complex relationship between Greek cities, the Attalid monarchy, and the rising power of Rome.
