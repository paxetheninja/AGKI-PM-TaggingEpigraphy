AI Analysis Summary

This inscription from Epidaurus, likely dating to the Roman Imperial period (2nd-3rd century AD), functions as a ritual calendar or a set of sacred regulations (lex sacra). It meticulously describes the daily liturgical duties of various officials, primarily the Pyrophoros (fire-bearer), who is tasked with tending to the altars and lighting lamps at specific hours. The text maps out a ritual procession through the sanctuary, visiting the Metroon, the Aphrodision, and the Propylaia, while making offerings to a diverse pantheon including Asclepius, Apollo, the Anakes, and Pan. The presence of terms like 'spondai' (libations) and 'thymiaterion' (incense burner) confirms its focus on ritual practice.
